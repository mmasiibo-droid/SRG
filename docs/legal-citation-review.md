# Legal citation review

**Checked:** 4 October 2026  
**Purpose:** source-check citations used in this portfolio, not legal advice or
a determination that any law applies to SRG.

## European Union GDPR — citation checked

The primary source reviewed was the official [EUR-Lex text of Regulation
(EU) 2016/679](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj).

- **Article 3** is the territorial-scope provision. It covers processing in
  the context of an EU establishment and certain processing by a controller
  or processor outside the EU relating to offering goods or services to people
  in the EU or monitoring their behaviour there. Actual applicability depends
  on facts not established by this project.
- **Articles 5–6** address processing principles and lawfulness; **12–22**
  cover transparency and data-subject rights; **25** data protection by
  design/default; **28** processors; **30** records of processing; **32**
  security; **35** data-protection impact assessment; and **44–49**
  international transfers. These references match the cited subjects.
- **Article 33** requires a controller to notify the competent supervisory
  authority without undue delay and, where feasible, within 72 hours after
  becoming aware of a personal-data breach, unless the breach is unlikely to
  result in a risk to individuals. If notification is later than 72 hours,
  reasons for delay must accompany it. A processor must notify its controller
  without undue delay. **Article 34** separately addresses communication to
  affected individuals where the breach is likely to result in a high risk,
  subject to the exceptions in that Article.

The portfolio now states the risk thresholds and does not present 72 hours as
an unconditional rule. The GDPR clock must not be applied to Uganda processing
unless the Regulation's territorial conditions are met.

## Uganda — framework identified; section-level citation not verified

The framework named in the portfolio is the **Data Protection and Privacy
Act, 2019 (Act 9 of 2019)**. The [Personal Data Protection Office (PDPO)
website](https://pdpo.go.ug/) is the official source location identified for
Uganda privacy guidance. However, the Act link exposed from that site returned
the site's application shell rather than readable statute text in this
verification pass; the [PDPO-hosted Act link](https://pdpo.go.ug/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/Data-Protection-and-Privacy-Act-2019.pdf)
could not be independently inspected.

Accordingly, this review does **not** certify Uganda section numbers,
subordinate-instrument provisions, current PDPO guidance, or any breach
notification deadline. The report deliberately states no Uganda deadline and
does not borrow the GDPR's 72-hour period. Before submission or operational
use, check the current Gazette/official consolidated Act, applicable
regulations, and PDPO guidance with a qualified Ugandan privacy professional
or counsel. Update the compliance register only after matching each claimed
duty to the current primary text.

## Effect on the deliverable

The verified GDPR article references and the Uganda source-access limitation
are documented in the portfolio and compliance register. The legal assessment
remains a release gate: no processing-role analysis, registration status,
lawful basis, transfer mechanism, or notification decision has been verified
for actual SRG processing.
